# Ranking tables

Accuracy and latency, ranked, with the two architecture families kept separate.
Generated by `scripts/ranking_tables.py` — regenerate rather than edit by hand.

All accuracy figures are **exact-IoU**, recomputed from the prediction dumps. Ultralytics'
own mAP scores OBB with ProbIoU and runs **+0.175 optimistic** on this dataset, so its
numbers are not used anywhere here.

**A hard capacity floor sits between `pico` and `n`** — a ~9× jump in mAP for roughly
twice the parameters. atto, femto and pico do not learn the task at all; their AUC is at
chance and they are excluded from every aggregate in this study.

**Backbone is a confound at the top.** s/m/l/x are DINOv3-pretrained while atto…n are
HGNetv2, so a DEIMv2 win in the upper rows is *foundation-model pre-training + a DETR
head*, not architecture alone. The clean architectural comparison against YOLO26 is the
HGNetv2 line, where `deimv2-n` (0.7922) sits below the best YOLO26 cell (0.8373).

## 4. DEIMv2 — latency ranking (8 variants)

This table is mostly empty, and the emptiness is a result rather than an omission.

| variant | Core ML export | burst median | sustained (15 min) | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| deimv2-n | converts, verified 0.568 px | 4.80 | **14.95** (cold) | **breach @ 0s** |
| deimv2-s | converts, **output wrong** (259 px) | — | — | not reportable |
| deimv2-m | converts, **output wrong** (260 px) | — | — | not reportable |
| deimv2-l | converts, **output wrong** (213 px) | — | — | not reportable |
| deimv2-x | converts, **output wrong** (277 px) | — | — | not reportable |
| deimv2-atto | not exported | — | — | below capacity floor |
| deimv2-femto | not exported | — | — | below capacity floor |
| deimv2-pico | not exported | — | — | below capacity floor |

**Only `n` can be timed honestly.** The four DINOv3 variants convert to Core ML *without
error* and then produce boxes 213–277 px wrong; timing them would report the speed of a
model that does not work, so the exporter refuses. The three below the capacity floor
were never exported because they do not learn the task.

**And `n` fails anyway.** Its 4.80 ms burst is the fastest single figure in the study,
but ~20–30 s of continuous inference takes it to ~15 ms, where it stays — 50% over
budget, and it cannot trade resolution for speed because it is fixed at 640 px.

So DEIMv2 leads on accuracy and has **no deployable configuration**. YOLO26 is
recommended for sustained latency and toolchain reliability, not for better accuracy.
